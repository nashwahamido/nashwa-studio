Case study · Web app

Atlasphere

Plan trips together, in real time. A group travel-planning web app where friends vote on activities, build a shared itinerary and chat as they go.

My role
Development lead
Team
Four-person MSc team, Trinity College Dublin
Timeline
Jan – Apr 2026
Stack
React, Vite, Node.js, Express, MySQL, Socket.io, deployed on Railway
Links
Live app · GitHub

The problem

Planning a trip with friends usually sprawls across a group chat, a maps tab and three half-finished notes. Decisions get lost, and nobody can see the plan taking shape together. We wanted one place where a group plans in real time and everyone sees the same thing at once.

Starting a trip

A new group trip begins with three quick questions, one per screen: where you’re going, which cities, and for how long. That’s all the app needs to set up the shared calendar and start recommending places.

What I did

I led development on a four-person team and built the parts that make it feel live:

  • A drag-and-drop itinerarywith resizable time blocks, synced across everyone in the group over Socket.io, so one person’s change appears instantly for the rest.
  • Group chat and votingso a group can discuss and decide on activities, with the winning picks feeding straight into the itinerary.
  • The accounts and comms layeremail verification, password reset and group invites, plus server-side image resizing for profile and group photos.
  • Place recommendationsfor the chosen city, each with a photo, pulling from external APIs.

A problem that stuck with me

Our host (Railway) blocks outbound SMTP ports, so email verification silently failed in production. I moved the whole mailing system to Brevo’s HTTP API, which fixed it: the kind of debugging and deployment call that only shows up once something is actually live.

Why it matters for my work

Atlasphere was my first full-stack build, and it’s where I learned to take a product from an empty repo to something deployed and genuinely usable, working across front end, back end and real-time sync. It’s the other half of how I work: not just designing an interface, but building the system behind it.
